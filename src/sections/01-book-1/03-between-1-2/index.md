- **Spot** the difference between output that sounds like you and output that only sounds polished

## Voice Personalization, Part 2

In Session 1 you described your voice. Now let's see if it holds up. A profile that works on a sample paragraph doesn't always survive a real task, the same way a suit that fits in the store might pinch once you sit down in it.

1. Pick two real things you need to write this week. An email to a customer and a social post work well, but anything real will do.
2. Start a new conversation, give Claude your profile from Session 1, and ask it to draft both.
3. Read each draft out loud. Mark every spot where you think "I'd never say that."
4. Tell Claude what was off and why. Be specific, so instead of "make it friendlier" say "I don't use exclamation points, and I'd never call a client 'friend'."
5. Ask Claude to update your profile with what it learned, and save the new version.

You're done when you have an updated profile and at least one draft you'd send with only light edits.

### Two Oaks Landscaping: 2026 Year in Review

**From:** Dana Whitfield and Marcus Bell, owners\
**To:** our crew, our accountant, and everyone who's listened to us talk about grass all year\
**Date:** January 2027

What a year. 2026 was the best year Two Oaks has ever had. Revenue grew 18% over 2025, we brought on 56 new customers, and for the first time we finished the year with more work than we could handle. Here's a quick look back, and a few decisions we need to make before spring.

**The spring discount worked.** For the first time, we offered 10% off spring cleanups booked before March 15. We announced it in our March newsletter, and the phone lit up. We finished 80 spring cleanups in March and April, up from 56 the spring before, and 62 of them used the discount. March alone brought in 21 new customers, more than any month we've ever had.

**Mowing is our steady backbone.** We went from 34 mowing contracts to 39. Those contracts pay every month from spring through fall, rain or shine, and they carried us through the summer when cleanup work dried up.

**Hardscape is where the growth is.** Our average hardscape project more than doubled this year. The big one was the patio and retaining wall we built for a restaurant in Germantown in August. At $18,500 it was our largest job ever, and we want more like it.

**The hard parts.** Winter is still rough. December through February brought in almost nothing, and we both picked up side work to cover it. March and April nearly broke us. Two people finishing 80 cleanups in eight weeks meant a lot of 12-hour days, and we turned down at least a dozen jobs.

**What we're deciding for 2027:**

1. Should we run the spring discount again?
2. Can we afford a third crew member from March through November? We figure that's about $28,000 for the season.
3. Should we go after more hardscape work?

Our monthly numbers for 2026 are below, with our 2025 totals for comparison. We'd appreciate a second set of eyes before we commit to anything.

Dana and Marcus

<summary>Proposed reading (optional, follow-up to Session 1): Your Profile in Other Tools (about 10 minutes)</summary>

**Why here:** Session 1 builds a profile in Claude. Learners who also use ChatGPT or Gemini at work will want to know where the same idea lives in those tools.

**Is it overkill?** Probably, as required work. This slot already has John's voice exercise and the Analyze This pre-read. It works better as optional further reading.

**Outline:**
- The idea carries over: any tool does better when it knows who you are.
- In ChatGPT: custom instructions, memory and Projects.
- In Gemini: Instructions for Gemini (all users) and Memory (needs a Google AI plan, US only). Gems become **skills** for personal accounts starting November 2026, so the reading should say skills.
- Rule of thumb: keep one written profile that you own, and paste it wherever you need it.
