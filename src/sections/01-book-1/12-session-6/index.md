## Goals

By the end of this session, you will be able to:

- **Build** Skills for more than one of your workflows
- **Test** a Skill on purpose, not just hope it works
- **Fix** a Skill that's used at the wrong time, or not used at all
- **Decide** when a task should stay a Skill and when it needs a human

## Review: How Did Your First Skill Do?

Share your Skill from Session 5 with your group. Did Claude use it when it should have? Did it ever use it when it shouldn't have? What did you have to check or fix in its output?

## Testing a Skill

A Skill that works once isn't finished. Think of it like a new recipe. The first time you make it, you follow every step and watch the pot. You don't know whether it's a good recipe until you've made it a few times, on a busy night, with whatever's in the fridge.

Test every Skill three ways:

- **The normal case** - the task it was built for. Does it do it right?
- **The near miss** - a task that's similar but different. Does Claude correctly leave the Skill out?
- **The messy case** - the right task with incomplete or odd input. Does it ask you, or does it guess?

## Exercise: Build, Test and Refine

1. Pick two more "good fit" workflows from your inventory.
2. Build a Skill for each, the same way you did in Session 5.
3. Run all three tests on each Skill.
4. When a Skill misfires, start with the description. Most of the time, the description is the reason Claude reaches for a Skill at the wrong time or not at all.
5. Check the output of every test. A Skill repeats what it was told, including its mistakes.

You're done when you have three Skills you've tested in all three ways, and you've fixed at least one problem you found.

## When Should It Stay Human?

Not everything should become a Skill, even if it could. Keep a human in the loop when:

- **The stakes are high** - money, legal matters or anything you can't undo
- **The judgment is the job** - relationships, negotiation or a feel for the situation
- **The output is hard to check** - if you can't tell quickly whether it's right, you can't automate it safely

## Check-In

- Why is the description the first thing to fix when a Skill misfires?
- Why is a Skill's mistake more expensive than a one-off prompt's mistake?
- If a teammate wanted to use one of your Skills, what would they need to know before trusting it?
